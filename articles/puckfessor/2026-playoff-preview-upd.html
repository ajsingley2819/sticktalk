2026 NHL Playoff Preview: The Matchups

By The Puckfessor  |  April 10, 2026  |  Est. reading time: 5 minutes

Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to the 2026 NHL playoff preview from yours truly — The Puckfessor. From men's and women's US dueling gold medals to golden boy Jack Hughes not knowing how to properly use a steak knife, the season has been a whirlwind. The NHL playoff format is simple but controversial: two conferences, four divisions — the top three seeds in each division automatically qualify, plus the two teams with the best remaining records in each conference. Eight teams per conference, sixteen total. I could talk about the entirety of the season all day, but let's get down to the nitty gritty.

Pittsburgh Penguins vs Philadelphia Flyers Pens in 7

A tale as old as time. Let's talk about who I just said — Pittsburgh and Philly, two teams that at the beginning of the year were being laughed at. Now look who's laughing: neither of them, because in the words of Sidney Crosby, "I don't like any guy on their team," and that just about sums up this relationship. The young guns of the Flyers look to push the old Penguins down the stairs. Not going to happen. Pens in 7.

Carolina Hurricanes vs Ottawa Senators Canes in 5

Hang the banner — Carolina has won yet another Eastern Conference regular season championship. I think I speak for all of us when I say I am sick of seeing this team absolutely smother teams in the first two rounds of the playoffs. The games are flat out boring, which is exactly how Rod wants them. On the other side of the ice, though, stand the Senators. The Senators have had maybe the most interesting season of all time. All the good this young core and offense brought, and yet the defense, the goaltending, and the off-ice noise had this team stuck in a well the entire season. Good on them for sticking it out and making the playoffs. Canes in 5.

Tampa Bay Lightning vs Montreal Canadiens Tampa in 5

Montreal is truly an awesome team. The lights have truly guided Cole Caufield home this year — 52 goals in the regular season. Think of this team as a pyramid: Caufield is one base, Slafkovsky the other, and the whole rest of the team is stacked on top. They will topple against this giant. The past two years were nothing short of tough for Tampa, running into Florida in the first round. They've continued to have a hell of a team since their Cup runs, but they kept running into giants. This year, they are the giant, and it's going to be a tall task for anyone to climb the mountains in Tampa. Tampa in 5.

Buffalo Sabres vs Boston Bruins Sabres in 5

Boston, TD Garden, supposed season from hell — and into the playoffs. You made it, Bruins fans. Your Stanley Cup window has come and gone, but this was an awesome season for you. Unfortunately, you drew the mighty Sabres. This team is crazy! How do you go from a 14-year playoff drought and last place in the conference to second overall in one season, in like 48 games?? That just doesn't happen if you're sane — but as we all know, "Nobody circles the wagons like the Buffalo Bills." Put that team on skates and it's the same energy: the atmosphere, the mindset, the people. All of it. And I am here for it. Bills — er, Sabres — in 5.

"The East is a gauntlet this year. Eight legitimate teams. Whoever comes out is battle-tested and ready to win a Cup." — The Puckfessor

Edmonton Oilers vs Anaheim Ducks EDM in 6

Anaheim has had a great season with a little help from their fellow division colleagues in Edmonton and Vegas. Nobody wanted to lead this pillow-fight division, so Anaheim just hung in there long enough to earn a playoff spot. And honestly? This misfit of players is actually working. Trouba and Gudas on the same team — yuck. Edmonton is Edmonton. I have a feeling they are going to be rolling these playoffs, but I thought that last year and they had games that looked like everyone should be going to the Hall of Fame — then the next night Bouchard wouldn't even have the worst turnover. EDM in 6.

Vegas Golden Knights vs Utah Mammoth Vegas in 4

Utah, you asked for hockey and you got it — playoff hockey at that. Unbelievable season, and this team is a ton of fun. The Mammoth mascot on the Zamboni is genuinely one of the coolest things I've seen in the league, and I'm shocked nobody thought of it sooner. I would have been drooling as a kid. But I have a feeling that however odd their season has been, Vegas returns home for Game 1 and slaps that mammoth so hard it'll be spitting out tusks after the opening whistle. Vegas in 4.

Dallas Stars vs Minnesota Wild Wild in 7

Here's the controversy of the first round — two of the top five teams in the league meeting before Memorial Day. Billy Guerin went shopping and came back with a handbag that cost the future. Honestly? Who cares — Quinn Hughes is amazing and he's been proving exactly why he was worth every asset. Dallas is the same old Dallas: they can't keep getting away with this, but somehow they do. Their mix of young and old just makes sense. Robertson — or Robison — or however you spell it is unreal. Wait, that's not the same guy? There are two of them?? Unfortunately, I think it might take three of them to take down Minny. Wild in 7.

Colorado Avalanche vs Los Angeles Kings Avs in 4

Good for the Kings for getting in, honestly. Kopitar has been such an awesome ambassador for the game of hockey, on and off the ice. Unfortunately, he has been sentenced to death by firing squad. The Colorado Avalanche are not fair. I'll say it plainly: they are better than their 2022 team. I said it. That team only lost four games the entire playoffs — four — and I believe this group is even better. The road is significantly harder now, but it starts here. Avs in 4.

"The West is Colorado's to lose. The only question is whether Dallas, Minnesota, or Vegas has enough to make it interesting past round two." — The Puckfessor
